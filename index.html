Business Email Compromise Training and Awareness: a role-based competency framework for the financial sector. Financial institutions move billions of dollars every day, on the strength of one thing: trust. In finance, large payments are routine, and staff are trained to act fast on instructions.

Business email compromise turns that trust into a weapon. It rarely looks like an attack. A criminal pretends to be someone you trust, and asks for something routine, by email. Then by a cloned voice, one that sounds exactly like someone you know. Or on a deepfake video call, where even the face looks right.

In 2025, people in the United States reported three billion US dollars in losses to the FBI. Worldwide, reported losses passed fifty-five billion. Add regulator pressure and access to client data, and finance becomes a prime target.

That is why this training is built around six roles, where one decision can move money or data. Executives. Accounts payable. HR and payroll. Customer service. IT. Administration. Every attack pulls the same human levers: Cialdini's six principles of persuasion.

My framework teaches three steps. Detect the attempt. Verify it somewhere the attacker cannot reach. And contain the damage if something goes wrong.

Learners watch a scenario, spot the red flags in a realistic email, make a decision, and see where they stand. The impact: one habit, decisions not slides, psychology made visible, and speed when it counts. Pause. Verify. Report.

About this project

This site is part of a capstone research project (CYBR7901 and CYBR7902) in the Master of Cybersecurity (Cyber Defence) at The University of Queensland. It supports the Role-Based BEC Competency Framework (RBCF): six training modules, one for each role most often targeted by business email compromise.

It is written for staff in financial-sector organisations, with Australian examples and regulation first. Laws, contacts and reporting services differ by country and industry, so the modules should be adapted before they are used elsewhere.
